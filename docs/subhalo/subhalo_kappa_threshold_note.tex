\section{What is being computed}

The subhalo population of a host is held \emph{fixed} at the production floor
$\psi_{\rm min}=m_{\rm floor}/M$ --- every subhalo is always sampled. A separate knob
$\kthrs$ then acts on each clump's \emph{actual convergence at the ray},
\begin{equation}
  \kappa_c(m,d), \qquad d=\lvert \mathbf{y}-\mathbf{R}_{\rm clump}\rvert
  \ \text{(lens-plane separation)},
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{y}$ is the \emph{ray impact parameter} --- the lens-plane offset of the line
of sight from the host centre --- and $\mathbf{R}_{\rm clump}$ is the projected clump
position. Two distances must be kept apart: $y$ measures ray-to-host-centre, $d$ measures
ray-to-clump. The threshold acts on $d$, but the distribution of $d$ depends on $y$ through
$f(d\,|\,y)$, which is what couples them. Every moment below is conditional on $y$; the
quoted numbers are area-weighted over the aperture,
\begin{equation}
  \langle X\rangle=\int_{0}^{r_{\max}}X(y)\,q(y)\,dy,
  \qquad q(y)=\frac{2y}{r_{\max}^{2}},
\end{equation}
i.e.\ uniform ray density across the disc of radius $r_{\max}$, the radius at which the
host's own convergence falls to the counting threshold $\kappa_{\rm thr}$
($r_{\max}=6705\,$kpc at the fiducial host).
dropping the clump from the sum whenever $\kappa_c<\kthrs$. This is the same rule the
\emph{host} halos already obey: the \texttt{r\_thr} table in \texttt{cpp/subhalo.cpp} is
precisely ``keep the object while its convergence at the ray exceeds $\kappa_{\rm thr}$''.
The two thresholds are therefore directly comparable, which a $\psi_{\rm min}$ (mass-floor)
sweep is not.

The cut does \emph{not} commute with a mass cut: a massive clump far from the ray is
dropped while a light clump the ray nearly hits survives. It must therefore be applied
inside the clump--ray distance integral, not as a mass mask outside it.

Dropped clumps keep their mass in the smooth host (mass-conserving carve, scheme A), so
the host is built at $M-\Mret$ where $\Mret$ is the \emph{retained} clump mass.

\section{Campbell moments with a threshold}

Clumps form a Poisson process with intensity $dN/d\ln\psi=\gamma\,\psi^{\alpha}
e^{-\beta\psi^{\omega}}$ ($\alpha=-0.82$, $\beta=50$, $\omega=4$; $\psi=m/M$), and the
clump--ray separation has the projected pdf $f(d\,|\,y)$ at ray impact parameter $y$.
For a Poisson sum $S=\sum_i g(x_i)$ Campbell's theorem gives $\langle S\rangle=\int\lambda g$
and ${\rm Var}(S)=\int\lambda g^{2}$. A threshold only \emph{restricts the domain}, so every
moment stays an exact two-dimensional quadrature with the indicator
$\Theta=\mathbf{1}[\kappa_c(m,d)\ge\kthrs]$ folded in:
\begin{align}
  \langle N_{\rm ret}|y\rangle      &= \int d\ln\psi\,\tfrac{dN}{d\ln\psi}\,\Pret , &
  \langle \kappa_{\rm sub}|y\rangle &= \int d\ln\psi\,\tfrac{dN}{d\ln\psi}\,
                                       \langle \kappa_c\Theta\rangle_d ,\\
  {\rm Var}(\kappa_{\rm sub}|y)     &= \int d\ln\psi\,\tfrac{dN}{d\ln\psi}\,
                                       \langle \kappa_c^{2}\Theta\rangle_d , &
  \langle \Mret|y\rangle            &= \int d\ln\psi\,\tfrac{dN}{d\ln\psi}\,m\,\Pret ,\\
  {\rm Var}(\Mret|y)                &= \int d\ln\psi\,\tfrac{dN}{d\ln\psi}\,m^{2}\,\Pret , &
  {\rm Cov}(\Mret,\kappa_{\rm sub}|y) &= \int d\ln\psi\,\tfrac{dN}{d\ln\psi}\,m\,
                                       \langle \kappa_c\Theta\rangle_d ,
\end{align}
with $\langle X\rangle_d\equiv\int dd\,f(d|y)\,X$ and $\Pret$ defined in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:pret}. \textbf{The mean carries $m$, the variance carries $m^{2}$.} That
single difference drives everything below.

\section{The host is a linear response}

The host convergence is $\kappa_{\rm host}=\kappa_{\rm NFW}(M-\Mret,\,y)$. Expanding about
the mean carve,
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\ \sigma_{\rm host}(y)=
  \left|\frac{\partial\kappa_{\rm NFW}}{\partial M}\right|
  \sqrt{{\rm Var}(\Mret|y)}\ }
  \label{eq:sighost}
\end{equation}
Two consequences worth stating explicitly.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $\Mret$ enters \emph{only through its variance}. A deterministic carve, however
        large, gives $\sigma_{\rm host}=0$: the host acquires scatter solely because
        $\sum_i m_i$ is redrawn in every realization. The host mass and the ray position
        are fixed; the realized subhalo mass is the only stochastic ingredient.
  \item The \emph{mean} carve $\langle\Mret\rangle$ shifts $\langle\kappa_{\rm host}\rangle$
        but touches $\sigma_{\rm host}$ only weakly, through the prefactor
        $\partial\kappa/\partial M$ evaluated at the reduced mass.
\end{enumerate}

Note also that the carve is \emph{not} spatial: the host is rebuilt as a fresh NFW of mass
$M-\Mret$ with $r_s,\rho_s$ from the $c(M,z)$ relation, rather than hollowed out at the
clump positions. Since the subhalo radial distribution is anti-biased, a spatially faithful
carve would remove mass preferentially from the outskirts and change $\kappa_{\rm host}$ at
small $y$ much less. This is a known approximation of scheme A.

\section{Where the threshold enters, and why $\sigma_{\rm host}$ is monotonic}

$\kappa_c(m,d)$ decreases monotonically in $d$, so the cut is equivalent to a
\emph{reach radius},
\begin{equation}
  \kappa_c(m,d)\ge\kthrs \iff d\le d_{\max}(m,\kthrs),
  \qquad
  \Pret(m,y)=\int_{0}^{d_{\max}(m,\kthrs)} f(d\,|\,y)\,dd .
  \label{eq:pret}
\end{equation}
The threshold appears in \emph{one place only}, the upper limit of Eq.~\eqref{eq:pret}.
Lowering $\kthrs$ increases $d_{\max}$ for \emph{every} mass, so $\Pret$ rises pointwise,
so the integrand of ${\rm Var}(\Mret)$ rises pointwise. Hence
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial\,\sigma_{\rm host}}{\partial\,\kthrs}\le 0
\end{equation}
with no numerics required. The limits follow at once:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\kthrs\to\infty$: $d_{\max}\to0$, $\Pret\to0$, ${\rm Var}(\Mret)\to0$, so
        $\sigma_{\rm host}\to0$. Nothing is carved, the host is exactly $M$ every
        realization, and it has no scatter.
  \item $\kthrs\to0$: $\Pret\to1$ and the variance saturates at the finite constant
        \begin{equation}
          {\rm Var}_\infty=\gamma M^{2}\!\int \psi^{\alpha+1}e^{-\beta\psi^{\omega}}\,d\psi ,
        \end{equation}
        convergent because $\alpha+1=0.18>-1$ at the low end and the exponential cuts off
        the high end.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Why the variance is top-heavy.} In $d\ln\psi$ the integrands scale as
\begin{equation}
  \text{mean:}\ \psi^{\alpha+1}=\psi^{0.18},
  \qquad
  \text{variance:}\ \psi^{\alpha+2}=\psi^{1.18}.
\end{equation}
Both rise toward large $\psi$, the variance far more steeply, so the carve \emph{scatter}
is set by the few heaviest subhalos while the carve \emph{mean} collects the whole swarm.
This is why the mean carved mass can double while $\sigma_{\rm host}$ moves by only a few
percent.

\paragraph{Why saturation is slow.} At large separation the projected NFW falls roughly as
$\kappa_c\propto d^{-2}$, so $d_{\max}\propto\kthrs^{-1/2}$: retention grows only as a power
law, and $\Pret\to1$ only once $d_{\max}$ exceeds the host's extent
($\sim r_{200}+r_{\max}\approx7500\,$kpc at the fiducial host). Hence $\sigma_{\rm host}$ is
still creeping upward at $\kthrs=10^{-6}$ rather than being flat.

\section{Decomposition of the total}

With the carve, host and subhalos are anti-correlated:
\begin{equation}
  {\rm Cov}(\kappa_{\rm host},\kappa_{\rm sub})
  = -\frac{\partial\kappa_{\rm NFW}}{\partial M}\,{\rm Cov}(\Mret,\kappa_{\rm sub}) < 0 ,
\end{equation}
since $\partial\kappa/\partial M>0$ and ${\rm Cov}(\Mret,\kappa_{\rm sub})>0$ (clump mass and
clump convergence rise together). A realization drawing a heavier clump population has more
$\kappa_{\rm sub}$ but a lighter host, hence less $\kappa_{\rm host}$. Therefore
\begin{equation}
  {\rm Var}_{\rm tot}={\rm Var}_{\rm host}+{\rm Var}_{\rm sub}
  +2\,{\rm Cov}(\kappa_{\rm host},\kappa_{\rm sub})
\end{equation}
can---and does---fall \emph{below} ${\rm Var}_{\rm sub}$. This ordering is a prediction of
mass conservation, not a numerical artifact: switch the carve off, leave the host at full
$M$, and the covariance vanishes, at which point $\sigma_{\rm tot}>\sigma_{\rm sub}$ as naive
addition suggests.

\section{Numerical results}

Fiducial host $M=10^{14}\,M_\odot$, $z_l=0.5$, $z_s=1$, $\psi_{\rm min}=10^{-7}$ fixed,
area-weighted over the aperture. Host counting threshold $\kappa_{\rm thr}=1.276\times10^{-4}$.

\noindent Component budget at $\kthrs=0$, in units of ${\rm Var}_{\rm sub}$:
$1+0.097-0.182=0.914$, i.e.\ $\sigma_{\rm tot}/\sigma_{\rm sub}=0.956$, correlation

$2.8$ of the total factor $3.1$ change in $\sigma_{\rm host}$ ($\simeq92\%$); freezing
${\rm Var}(\Mret)$ and letting only the host mass vary gives $8\%$.

\section{Caveats}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Equation~\eqref{eq:sighost} is a \emph{first-order} response. The carve fluctuation
        is not small and not Gaussian: ${\rm sd}(\Mret)/M=0.110$ with skewness $1.83$ at
        $\kthrs=0$, rising to skewness $\sim6$ at the host threshold, where the carve is
        essentially ``was one massive clump retained or not''. $\sigma_{\rm host}$ is thus
        the least trustworthy of the three curves; $\sigma_{\rm sub}$ is exact Campbell with
        no linearization and dominates the total.
  \item $\sigma_{\rm host}$ and $\sigma_{\rm sub}$ are not separately physical. The threshold
        defines where the split between ``explicitly realized clump'' and ``smooth host''
        falls, and moving it shuffles mass across that line, so both components must move.
        Only the total is meaningful, and the total is flat to $0.2\%$ at the host threshold.
  \item All scatter here is substructure-induced: a smooth deterministic halo at fixed $y$
        has none, which is why $\sigma_{\rm tot}\to0$ at large $\kthrs$. This is the
        single-host substructure contribution, not $\sigma_\kappa$ of the universe.
  \item Certified for $\sigma_\kappa$ and the mean only. Since the tail of $P(\ln\mu)$ is
        built from rare close encounters, and a $\kappa$ threshold is precisely a cut on
        encounter closeness, a JSD check on $P(\ln\mu)$ is required before adopting the
        threshold in production.
  \item Realizing the computational saving needs a sampler restructure. Testing
        $\kappa_c(m,d)\ge\kthrs$ per clump requires $m$ and $d$, so draw-and-reject would
        still instantiate all $3.4\times10^{4}$ clumps. The saving requires sampling from the
        \emph{restricted} intensity: ${\rm Poisson}(\langle N_{\rm ret}\rangle(y))$ clumps
        drawn from the retention-conditioned distribution, never instantiating the rejects.
  \item Numerics: grids converged to $\sim10^{-4}$ in $n_d$ and $n_{\rm mass}$; the baseline
        agrees with the independent $\psi_{\rm min}$ engine to $1.4\times10^{-5}$. The $n_y$
        aperture grid shifts all curves by a uniform $1.83\%$, which cancels in ratios.
\end{itemize}
